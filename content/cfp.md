## Call for abstracts

The workshop aims to bring together researchers interested in the broad area of synthesis of computing systems, interpreted inclusively. Tracks highlighted in 2026 were reactive synthesis, functional synthesis, and neuro-symbolic synthesis; other synthesis-related work is welcome.

Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

- Specification languages for synthesis
- Algorithms, complexity and decidability analysis
- Reproducible heuristics, implemented tools, experimental evaluations, case studies
- Benchmark descriptions and benchmark families
- Connections between verification and synthesis
- Synthesis by model learning; machine and reinforcement learning
- Inductive programming, repair, fault localization, testing
- Discovery of inductive invariants, parameter optimization, constraint solving, theorem proving
- Synthesis and large language models: synthesis for agentic AI, and agentic AI for synthesis. Contributions are expected to identify a specification or correctness criterion, explain how the learned component is integrated into a synthesis procedure, or state what the resulting procedure guarantees and how. Work using a language model as an end-to-end black box that emits code is not in scope.

## Submission details

- Extended abstracts of up to 3 pages in LNCS format.
- Submissions are judged on interest to the SYNT community. Overlap with prior work must be indicated but does not automatically disqualify a submission.
- Parallel submissions are allowed.
- There are no published proceedings. Selected contributions may be followed up with an archival or journal publication opportunity after the workshop.
