## Scope

The Workshop on Synthesis (SYNT) aims to bring together researchers interested in the broad area of synthesis of computing systems. It fosters the development of frontier techniques in automating the development of engineered systems where computation plays an important role, and is inclusive in its interpretation of the term "synthesis".

Contributions of interest include algorithms, complexity and decidability analysis, as well as reproducible heuristics, implemented tools, benchmark descriptions, and experimental evaluations. Topics include:

- Computation models: functional, reactive, hybrid, timed (and neurosymbolic) systems.
- Application domains: software, hardware, embedded, and cyber-physical systems.
- Specification forms: contracts, temporal logic, quantitative objectives, input/output examples.
- Underlying methods: formal methods, computer-aided verification, machine and reinforcement learning.
- Synthesis and large language models: synthesis for agentic AI and the use of agentic AI for synthesis. Contributions must identify a specification or correctness criterion, explain how the learned component is integrated into a synthesis procedure, or state what the resulting procedure guarantees and how. Work that uses a language model as an end-to-end black box emitting code is out of scope.
- Related areas: inductive programming, repair, fault localization, testing, discovery (synthesis) of inductive invariants, parameter optimization, constraint solving, and theorem proving (SMT, superposition-based, inductive, higher-order; automated lemma generation and knowledge discovery).

The workshop also hosts the reactive synthesis competition (SYNTCOMP) and the syntax-guided synthesis competition (SyGuS); contributions describing tools and benchmarks submitted to these competitions are encouraged.
